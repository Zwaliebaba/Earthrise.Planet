# Earthrise — Design review 2026-10-18

This is a review of `Design/MVP.md`, Draft 0.2 (2026-10-18), produced by the multi-agent review that `Design/REVIEW-PROMPT.md` describes: seven lens reviewers, one adversarial verifier for each, and this synthesis. It is advisory, and every decision remains the owner's. The open questions it raised are O-16 onward in MVP.md §12.

## 1. Verdict

**Stage 1.** The rules can produce an hour worth playing, but they do not yet ensure one.

The core asymmetry is structural. With no forward spawn (D-17), an attacker who dies is out of the fight for 64.5–94.5 s. A defender is out for about 16.5 s, because they respawn beside the points until every one is lost (D-28). So a contested attack needs roughly 1.65–2.2 times the defenders' committed numbers, a human who attacks spends half to two-thirds of their time walking or waiting, and no director lever reaches the gap (L1-01).

Around that core, the capture rules give no answer, or two, in cases that a three-faction war on one- and two-point bases produces every few minutes:

- what "majority" means, and what the timer does when nobody has one (L1-07);
- whether a downed body holds a point (L1-04);
- whether enemies may walk into a spawn room (L1-03);
- whether a lost spawn comes back (L5-02);
- whether the stairs to a hardened plinth can be blown away (L3-03).

The voxel pillar is the game's reason to exist, yet nothing makes a breach beat a doorway (L3-02), nobody owns the pool (L3-05), and §10 cuts the pillar's gameplay first (L3-01). The human fights near their allies rather than with them: they have no squad, no shared picture, and no sign on any map of where the fighting is (L4-01, L3-10, L1-06). §9 would pass all of it, because every flow target can be met while attackers walk and bases change hands empty (L1-02, L3-11).

None of this needs a redesign. Nearly every fix is a sentence in §6, an authoring rule beside D-39, a staging spawn or a metric, and each is cheap if written before the milestone that codes it: M3 for the downed state, M5 for perception and the cut order, M6 for most of the rest.

Confidence: medium. The arithmetic for walks, timers and parity follows directly from the stated numbers, and verifiers re-ran it. The war-level model that places the war's regime (an empty carousel, a fought churn or a lock) depends on a commander policy that §6.11 does not state. It also freezes points more strictly than §6.4 does, so the M6 soak and the owner's own sessions are what settle whether the hour is fun.

**Stage 2.** No MVP decision was found to be a one-way door. Every finding that threatened only Stage 2 was downgraded by its verifier for exactly that reason. The path stays open through:

- the N-faction data model (D-05, R23);
- bots that act only through the command interface (R19, R20);
- voxel edits replicated as integer operations (R28, R29);
- a late-join chunk diff that makes a save nearly free (§7.4);
- server-authoritative hits (D-10);
- one server process per continent (D-36).

The risks are of another kind. The audience the live game courts is shrinking and captive. PlanetSide 2's Steam average fell by 39–47% between January 2025 and August 2026, it consolidated first its European and then its North American servers by May 2025, and PlanetSide Arena closed four months after launch for want of players. At any population a solo developer can plausibly reach, most continent seats will be bots. The live game is therefore a bot-backfilled war whether or not the design says so (L4-08), and its reason to exist next to Battlefield 6, Foxhole and Ravenfield is the build-and-breach pillar that §10 cuts first (L3-01). The rule gaps above become exploits once humans read them adversarially: bodies left on plinths, spawn rooms camped, defenders blowing their own stairs, one player emptying the faction's pool.

Confidence: medium-low. It rests on comparables known only from search-engine summaries (no agent could open a page), on population trackers that count Steam only and so miss PlanetSide 2's own launcher and consoles, and on estimates of daily population troughs.

**The weakest point.** The attack. Under D-17 and D-28 an attacker's death costs about 80 s and a defender's about 16 s. Three lenses reached the consequence independently:

- L1's presence model puts parity at 1.65–2.2 times the garrison.
- L2's war model finds its capture-rate cliff where attackers are about twice the garrison.
- L4's squad model has attackers arriving one at a time every 8–17 s into defenders who respawn on the points.

The walk is fixed by the layout (§5.1, D-29), and no lever offsets it. The director's ±3 s of respawn is 3–5% of the attacker's cycle and 18% of the defender's, so it mostly helps whoever is defending. Captures therefore come from commanders catching garrisons under-committed, not from fights won, and the human who chooses to attack spends most of their hour walking, which is the "conquer" in the pillar statement. §9 cannot see it.

The cheapest fix that keeps D-28's strength is a staging spawn on each lattice link. It brings parity to 1.15–1.27 times, inside the director's bounds, and it is also the spawn-point abstraction that Stage 2's mobile spawns need (O-16).

## 4. Findings

### Stage 1 — major

#### L3-10 (+L4-02) — R20 does not define what a faction knows: bots already read more than the rule allows, and the human gets less than their allies

*Stage 1 · major · structural · decide before M5 · feeds O-21*

**Claim.** R20's exception covers "lattice, ownership and capture progress" (§6.11). The same section assumes more than that:

- the commander reads "its own soldiers' positions";
- each base's task list holds "damaged areas to repair", which is a diff of blueprint against current voxels;
- §6.9 and D-31 have every bot check a blast against allies, without saying whether that means the allies it perceives or their true positions.

Navigation is built from the true grid and reflects every edit within two ticks, map-wide (§7.5). A squad 400 m away therefore routes around a wall it never saw sealed. None of this is on any player's map: §6.3 and §6.12 show no allies, no damage, no downed markers and no spotting. As written, R20 forbids what the design requires, and the bots know more about their own faction than the human does.

**Scenario.** The human, an assault, is downed behind a crate inside 3·1. A blue medic bot 15 m away, round a corner, did not see it happen, and under a strict reading of R20 it cannot know, so the human gives up and walks back. Or a blue assault bot plants a charge while the human crouches on the far side, out of its view cone, and kills them (D-31). Or 32 blue bots have seen twelve amber bots massing behind 1·2's north wall, and the human walks into them knowing nothing.

**Recommendation.** Define one faction picture, meaning what every soldier of a faction knows, and make it both R20's exception and the content of the minimap and deploy screen. It holds:

- lattice, ownership and capture progress, as now;
- allied positions;
- downed allies within N m;
- enemies spotted by any ally in the last N s, through a spot command in the command interface, so that bots spot the way the human does (R19);
- for owned bases, damage against blueprint.

Record separately whether navigation over current voxels is an accepted exception, or whether it plans on the blueprint plus what the squad has perceived. Propose the picture as a C-nn. Cost: a spot command, minimap markers and a faction-filtered event. It exposes no new data, because relevance already sends every nearby entity (§7.4).

**Settled by.** Playtest: the share of the human's downs that end in a revive, and their deaths to allied explosives. Soak: an R20 audit of AI decision inputs against the defined picture.

**Verifiers.** L4's verifier confirmed it and widened it to navigation. It noted that a downed human may respawn at any time (§6.2), so the defect is the missing revive marker, not a forced wait. L3's verifier noted that perception includes explosions within earshot across a base no more than 160 m wide, so the omniscience the human would notice is small. The rule gap stands.

#### L1-01 (+L4-05) — Decision challenge, D-17: an attacker's death costs about 80 s and a defender's about 16 s, and no lever closes the gap

*Stage 1 · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-16*

**Claim.** Spawning only at a warpgate or an owned base (D-17), with defenders keeping their spawn until every point falls (D-28), makes a death several times more expensive for the attacker. Contested attacks need roughly 1.65–2.2 times the defenders' committed numbers. A human attacker spends half to two-thirds of their time out of contact, and squads with no regroup rule dissolve into a trickle.

**Scenario.** Twenty minutes in, the human (faction 1, medic) joins two bot squads attacking 2·1 from 1·2, 450 m away. They sprint 67.5 s across open ground and arrive about fifteen strong against a garrison of about nine. Each defender who dies is back on the points 16.5 s later; each attacker who dies is back 79.5 s later. Within three minutes the attack is arriving one soldier every 8–17 s into a full garrison. The human's lives last about 40 s in contact and cost about 80 s each, so an hour of attacking is about 20 minutes of fighting.

**Evidence.** Sprint speed is 500 m in 75 s, 6.67 m/s (§5.1). Time out of the fight per death:

- Attacker: 10 s respawn (§6.3) + about 2 s on the deploy screen + a 52.5–82.5 s walk = 64.5–94.5 s.
- Defender: 10 + 2 + 4.5 s = 16.5 s, with the spawn room assumed 30 m from the points; the design does not state that distance.

If each soldier present downs enemies at a rate of 1/life, equal presence at the base needs Na/Nd ≥ (1 + Ta/life)/(1 + Td/life). That is 1.95 at 450 m with a 50 s life, 1.65 with an 80 s life, and 2.17 at 550 m.

At a 40 s life, the human is in contact 40/119.5 = 33% of the time when attacking and 40/56.5 = 71% when defending. The director's ±3 s respawn lever is 3.2–4.7% of the attacker's cycle and 18% of the defender's.

Two models built separately agree. L2's war model puts its capture-rate cliff where attackers are about twice the garrison. L4's squad model (lives of 20–45 s, 0–30% of deaths revived) has 1.4–3.9 of 8 attackers present at once, against 4.6–6.0 of 8 defenders.

The cost is not priced. D-17's Consequence names only the defenders' rule. D-28's says "a defence does not have to walk in". §5.1 calls the walk "the cost of having no vehicles", as though it fell on both sides.

**Recommendation.** Supersede D-17: a soldier spawns at the warpgate, an owned base, or a fixed staging point on a lattice link. Place one hardened staging spawn at each link end, 125–150 m from the opposite base. The owner of that end may use it only while the opposite base belongs to another faction; optionally it is suppressed while enemies are within about 30 m. Keep D-28.

With staging spawns:

- an attacker's time out falls to 30.8–34.5 s;
- parity falls to 1.15–1.27 times, inside the director's soldier bound;
- the respawn lever becomes about 9–10% of the attacker's cycle;
- a human attacker's contact share at a 40 s life rises from 33% to 54–57%.

Add a squad regroup rule (L4-05): respawned members gather at a rally point and advance at 5 of 8, or after 30 s, so that pressure arrives in waves. Regrouping changes how the shortfall looks, not its size, since waiting lengthens the cycle.

Cost:

- 18 authored spawn points: both ends of the six links between bases, and the warpgate end of the six warpgate links;
- a deploy-screen option;
- a spawn-point abstraction, in which a spawn is an entity with an owner and a validity rule. Stage 2's mobile spawns need that abstraction, and §6.13 does not reserve it.

It forecloses the march between bases as a feature. A variant closer to the pillar is an engineer-built, breachable staging module on an authored link slot. It needs an exception to §6.7's "only inside bases the faction owns".

**Settled by.** M6 soak: share of soldier-time in contact, split by attacker and defender; committed attacker-to-defender ratio in successful and failed attacks; share of captures with five or more defenders present in the two minutes before the sweep; attacker inter-arrival times and lone deaths. Also a one-hour human playtest measuring time in contact while attacking.

**Verifier.** Downgraded from blocker to major. "Contested attacks do not finish" does not follow from the lens's own model. Solved for an 8-soldier garrison, defender presence falls to 2.9 at 2.5 times the garrison and to 0.2 at 3 times. A faction that leaves 6–8 soldiers at each of two bases can field 17–21 attackers, which is 2.1–3.5 times one garrison. So concentrated attacks finish, and the binding constraint is how well commanders concentrate. Comparing local ratios with the director's faction-wide bounds is a category error. The structural core stands: the walk is fixed by the layout, and nothing in the design shortens the attacker's return. L4-05 was confirmed major, and its verifier asked for it to be merged here.

#### L3-11 — §9 counts voxel actions, not whether they changed a fight

*Stage 1 · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-19*

**Claim.** §9's only target for the voxel pillar is that bots "carry out builds, repairs and breaches every hour", and one occurrence of each meets it. Nothing measures whether a breach was walked through, whether a module changed a capture, or whether the pool ever constrained anyone. Yet breaching (§5.3), the commander's bet (D-32) and the territory–building loop (D-25) are all claimed to matter.

**Scenario.** At M8 every §9 target is green. That hour's breaches were two walls at 3·2 that nobody walked through. The modules lasted until the first frag, the repairs were sandbag nicks restoration would have fixed anyway, and every pool sat at its cap. From §9, the owner cannot tell whether the voxel pillar matters, which is exactly what §10 needs to know before cutting it.

**Recommendation.** Add four flow targets for the two-hour soak (the numbers are proposals):

- (a) in at least 25% of captures, a point is flipped by an attacker who passed through an explosion-made opening in the previous 60 s;
- (b) bases with half or more of their slots filled when an attack began are captured at least 15 percentage points less often than bare ones;
- (c) no faction's pool sits at its cap, or below the cheapest module, for more than 50% of the time;
- (d) a completed module survives a median of at least 60 s under attack.

All four come from the R27 events already logged (§7.9), plus two event fields: entry through an opening, and module ids on voxel removals.

**Settled by.** The metrics themselves. They also settle L3-01, L3-02, L3-04 and L3-06.

**Verifier.** Confirmed major. The owner's own playtesting is a partial check, which keeps this short of a blocker. The thresholds are proposals, so they are tuning.

#### L4-01 — The war happens beside the human: they belong to no squad and nothing shows them what their faction is doing

*Stage 1 · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-21*

**Claim.** §6.11 forms squads of six to eight bots, and nothing assigns the human to one or counts them in its strength. The deploy screen (§6.3) and the minimap (§6.12) show no friendly objectives, squads or allies, and D-15 makes a ping cosmetic. Membership and information are not command, so neither D-15's Consequence nor §8's "squad or platoon command by the player" covers this.

**Scenario.** Minute 20. Faction 1's commander has sent three squads at 3·1 and one to hold 1·3, and the deploy screen shows none of it. The only active signal is red's capture progress at C, so the human walks about 400 m to C, meets ten red bots with no blue squad in sight, and dies alone. In their next life a blue squad spawns with them at 1·2 and moves off on its own route without waiting for them.

**Evidence.** 32 bots in squads of 6–8 is 4.0–5.3 squads. The human is 1/33 = 3.0% of their faction. A wrong guess costs a 52–82 s walk. Health does not regenerate except from a medic (§6.2), so a human who drifts away from bot medics fights on at partial health. Capture progress (§6.4) appears only once a point is already flipping, so it shows where a fight was, not where the faction is going.

**Recommendation.** Make the human a squad member without giving them command:

1. The deploy screen lists the commander's current objectives and the squad on each. The human picks one, or "free", and spawns at the owned spawn nearest that squad's rally point.
2. The squad counts the human in heal, revive, resupply and regroup.
3. The HUD marks their squad by shape and colour (D-41).
4. The commander counts the human as a soldier it knows about but does not control.

D-15 holds. Cost: a membership flag and one deploy-screen and HUD element. This is the same seam Stage 2 backfill needs (L4-08).

**Settled by.** Playtest: the share of session time the human spends within 50 m of at least three allied bots that share their objective, and their time from deploy to first engagement.

**Verifier.** Confirmed major. Bots do support the human when they are near: they revive and resupply any ally they perceive. The gap is that nothing brings the human and the bots together. Item 4 depends on the faction picture in L3-10.

### Stage 1 — minor

#### L4-11 — Soldier-against-soldier collision is unstated, and two soldiers cannot pass in a minimum doorway

*Stage 1 · minor · structural · decide before M2*

D-39 sets doorways at a minimum of 1.0 m, and the soldier box is 0.7 m wide (§6.6), so two soldiers abreast need 1.4 m. §9's "when not touching other soldiers" implies that soldiers do collide, and §7.5's local steering covers bots only. A blue bot that stops in a 1 m doorway to reload leaves the human stuck behind it, shot in the back. State in §6.6 that soldiers of one faction do not block each other (pass through, or a soft push), and decide separately whether enemies collide. Settled by a playtest measure: time spent blocked by allies at doorways. The verifier confirmed it and moved the deadline to M2, when shared movement lands; adding pass-through later is still not a rewrite.

#### L2-02 (+L4-09) — The commander's force split is undesigned, and it sets the war's regime

*Stage 1 · minor · tuning · decide before M6*

L2's model swept the garrison per exposed base, and captures per hour ran from 46 down to 0.5 as the garrison went from 2 to 10 soldiers. §9's floor of 6 per hour sits on that knee. §6.11 gives the commander no rule for where to attack or what to defend, and nothing behind §4's claim that "whoever looks strongest gets attacked from two directions".

The human-specific variant (L4-09) is ghost-capping. The human slips into empty small base 2·1 and holds it: a 12 s flip, then 45 s at double speed, 57 s in all. The commander learns of it only through capture progress.

Write the commander's allocation into §6.11 as data:

- a garrison fraction;
- a minimum garrison of 1–2 bots on any owned base linked to an enemy base;
- top-priority defence when an owned point starts to flip;
- a target term for the leader's bases, spread across them rather than piled on one.

Settled by an M6 soak sweeping the garrison fraction against captures per hour and contested share.

Both verifiers downgraded it to minor tuning. A band of garrison values (about 2–8 of 33) sits above the floor with fights. The model's cliff is sharpened by an artifact: it freezes a whole base whenever any second faction is anywhere inside it, which is stricter than §6.4's per-point rule. And under D-28, red can still spawn into 2·1 during the human's 12 s flip, so the real defence window is 12 s of spawning in, not a walk from the next base.

#### L2-05 — The director has no deadband, and its respawn lever lands on the human's own countdown

*Stage 1 · minor · tuning · decide before M6*

Seven bases never split evenly among three factions; the closest splits are 3-2-2, or 2-2-2 with C neutral. A proportional director is therefore almost always acting: 94–96% of the time in L2's model, at a bound about 82% of it. The respawn lever moves an attacker's share of time in the fight by only ±2–3%, yet it is the one director output the human sees on every death, and its trend term fires during their best streak.

Give the director a deadband: no adjustment while every faction holds 2–3 bases, and act on how long a lead lasts (for example four or more bases for over 10 minutes). Consider applying the respawn lever to bots only, or replacing it with commander target bias.

#### L1-04 (+L5-04) — The downed state is silent where it decides fights: presence on points, finishing, and when the respawn clock runs

*Both · major · structural · decide before M3 · feeds O-17*

**Claim.** §6.4 flips a point to a faction that "stands on it uncontested" and freezes it on "any enemy presence". §6.2 keeps a downed soldier as a revivable body for 30 s. A downed body fits "presence" but not "stands", and a naive presence query, over a soldier entity with a position inside the point volume, will count it. Nothing says whether a body can be finished. Nothing says whether the 10 s respawn runs from the moment of downing or from the choice to respawn.

**Scenario.** Minute 35. The human defends 1·3's single point against six F3 bots and is downed on the plinth. If their body counts as presence, the point stays frozen while they lie there, up to 30 s against a 12 s solo flip, and they can simply choose not to respawn. **Evidence.** A defender's cycle is about 5 s to run in, 30 s downed, and 12 s to respawn and deploy: about 47 s. The respawn clock matters as much:

- If it runs from the down, a soldier who waits 13 s or more for a revive pays nothing extra, and the director's lever never reaches them.
- If it runs from the choice, giving up after a full wait costs 40 s.

1. A downed soldier is not present for any rule that counts soldiers (control points, flip rate, restoration's calm) until revived.
2. A downed body takes no damage. If the owner wants revives to be deniable, explosive damage ends the window instead; that must then be a bot action (R19), and it should stay costly, because a revive is the attacker's main answer to the walk.
3. The respawn delay runs from the moment of downing, and when the window ends the soldier goes to the deploy screen with the delay already served.

Cost: writing it down. Running the delay from the down makes the first 10 s of waiting free, which slightly strengthens medics.

**Settled by.** The owner's ruling. Then, in soak: per-point frozen time split by cause (downed against living), with one log line per freeze; mean downed wait and revive rate by role.

**Scenario.** The human defends 2·3 (two points); F3 holds A and contests B. Two F3 bots whose route between A and B passes the spawn room door have it in their view cones. **Recommendation.** A hardened-core rule in §5.3:

- While its owner holds the spawn, a spawn room's doorways admit the owner and block enemy soldiers and enemy projectiles. A lethal no-entry volume is the alternative; it keeps one collision model and one navigation graph (§7.3, §7.5).
- Warpgates are closed to non-owners at all times.
- A newly spawned soldier is protected briefly, until they move or fire.
- Terminals serve only the base's owner.

#### L2-06 (+L6-03) — Decision challenge, D-09: the session has no ending, and the war is built to erase whatever the player achieves

*Both · major · structural · decide before M6 (soft)*

**Claim.** D-09's Consequence prices "no round reset" and acceptance by flow metrics. It does not price the player's side:

- §9 fails any faction holding 5 of 7 bases (71.4%) for 15 minutes, so 4 of 7 (57.1%) is the most a faction may hold for long;
- the director pushes against the leader even when the human made the lead (§6.10);
- D-11 keeps nothing.

So "conquer" has no reachable, acknowledged state within a session.

**Scenario.** Forty minutes in, the human's faction holds 4 of 7, and the director is moving soldiers, respawn and income towards the trailing two. An hour later the map is back to two or three bases each. The human quits at an arbitrary moment, nothing records that their faction held four, and next week starts from the same board.

**Evidence.** The arithmetic above, and the reference game's history:

- PlanetSide 2 switched on continent locking with the Hossin update on 26 June 2014: 94% territory or an alert win. PC Gamer called the feature "long-demanded".
- In 2017 (Critical Mass) it rebuilt locking around Victory Points. Daybreak called the old locking "confusing" and "abrupt" and aimed for "a climactic end-of-continent encounter".

- In 2020 the trigger moved to Empire Strength, and the winner still locked the continent.
- Foxhole's wars end on Victory Points, and the world resets.

**Recommendation.** Keep the endless map, and add a bounded closure inside it:

- an alert about every 45 minutes of play, lasting 20–30 minutes;
- scored on territory share at its end, with ties broken by captures during the window;
- announced, logged (R27) and shown on a session summary at quit;
- the war continues afterwards.

Decide whether the director freezes in an alert's last minutes. Restate §9's 70% line so it applies outside alert windows, and add "every faction wins at least one alert in six".

The cheaper and weaker alternative is the session summary alone, built from R27 events, with no change to D-09. Cost: a timer, a scoring rule, one event type, a deploy-screen line and a summary screen, with D-09 narrowing to "no war-level win state". The alert is also where Stage 2's continent lock attaches, and §2 already reserves continent locks.

**Settled by.** A playtest over five or more sessions: where the owner stops, their stated reason, and whether they play an alert window through to its end.

**Verifiers.** Split. L6's verifier confirmed major for Stage 1: the arc is missing, D-09 does not price it, and PlanetSide 2's history is new information. It corrected the scenario's fifth base (3·2 is not attackable from faction 1's holdings; it would be 2·3 or 3·1) and noted that §9 is an MVP target, so a later lock threshold above 70% conflicts with no structure. L2's verifier downgraded to minor: §2 reserves continent locks, the model's two-hour trace swings from (0,3,3) through (1,0,6) to (6,0,1), which is shape, and whether a solo hour needs closure is a playtest hypothesis.

This synthesis keeps major, because both verifiers agree that the payoff is missing and that D-09 does not price it. It is not put to the owner as an open question: the five-session playtest decides it, and the session summary costs little and should be built regardless. Recorded in §7.

#### L3-02 (+L4-06) — Nothing makes a breach beat walking through a door

*Both · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-22*

**Claim.** A breach costs walking up, planting, backing off out of the blast (D-31) and the fuse; a door costs only walking. At 6.67 m/s a 3–8 s breach is worth a 20–53 m detour, and in a base no more than 160 m across a door is usually nearer than that. Nothing tips the balance:

- no doors close (shield doors are under Later, §2);
- D-39 sets only a doorway's size;
- §6.11 charges "exposure" on breach edges alone, so a defended doorway looks cheaper than an undefended wall;
- nothing requires a point to be inside a building, or behind any wall.

§9 counts breaches map-wide per hour, so one irrelevant breach meets it.

**Scenario.** F2's eight-bot squad attacks point A of 1·2, inside a two-storey building. Its doorway is 25 m round the corner from the heavy wall facing the point. The door is cheaper, so navigation takes it every time, into F1 defenders who respawn beside the point (D-28). Across a six-hour soak, bots breach only where a blueprint happens to have no door nearby. If the human is at one of about three active fights, one breach an hour map-wide is one they see every three hours.

**Recommendation.**

1. A breach-relevance authoring rule beside D-39, checked offline with the shipping navigation code. For each control point, from each approach facing the lattice, either the walk without a breach is at least about 40 m longer than the walk with one breach through a heavy wall within the breach-edge thickness limit, or the door route passes a doorway in line of sight of a defender spawn exit or of the point.
2. Some build slots across openings, so that a wall module can close an entry and force a breach. L3-03's guaranteed route stays free of slots.
3. Define "exposure" as perceived threat and apply it to every edge, doors included: each squad keeps a decaying danger value on edges near enemies it has seen, and where its members died (R20).
4. Change §9's breach target from per hour to per attack (L3-11).

Cost: bases are designed around enclosed points; the check runs over seven blueprints; squads replan about once a second at squad level; and the threat term must be tuned so bots do not breach everywhere.

**Settled by.** Soak:

- breaches per capture;
- by base, the share of point flips in which the flipper passed through an explosion-made opening in the previous 60 s (a blueprint near zero fails the rule);
- the share of breaches at a wall where an ally died at a door within 30 m in the previous 60 s.

Playtest: breaches the human witnesses per hour.

**Verifiers.** Both confirmed major. Both pushed back on "the door every time": §6.11 never says ordinary edges are costed by distance alone, so threat-weighted costs are unspecified rather than excluded, and a 33–53 m detour is not rare in a 160 m base. The authoring half stands regardless. The smallest navigation fix is to apply the "exposure" term already in §6.11 to every edge.

#### L3-03 (+L5-05) — D-23 hardens the plinth but not the way to it

*Both · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-22*

**Claim.** Only spawn rooms, plinths, terminals and anchors are hardened (§5.4); stairs, ramps and floors are destructible shell (§5.3). The rest follows:

- a storey is at least 5 voxels, 2.5 m: 4 of headroom under D-39 plus a floor;
- a jump clears a 1.0 m ledge, with an apex of about 1.1 m;
- explosions only remove voxels, so a breach can never create a step up;
- attackers cannot repair, or place a ramp module, in a base they do not own (§6.7);
- restoration waits for 30 s with no enemy presence.

A point whose only route is destructible therefore becomes unreachable until its owner repairs. D-23's "the capture loop cannot be deleted" rests on an authoring discipline that nothing states: D-39 has a clearance rule but no reachability rule.

**Scenario.** Stage 2: point B of 2·3 is on an upper floor reached by one staircase, and F2's defenders blow their own stairs. Explosions do not check faction (§6.8). F3 holds A but cannot reach B, so on a two-point base it never holds a majority. F2 keeps its spawn indefinitely under D-28 and has every reason not to repair. Stage 1: an F3 breach through the stairwell wall drops the stairs above it (D-26), and F3's squads are recorded as stuck.

**Recommendation.** An access authoring rule beside D-39, checked offline by removing every destructible voxel and filling every slot, then searching the walkable-span graph:

- every control point's capture area and every spawn-room exit joins the base boundary by at least one walkable route whose floors, steps and ramps are hardened or terrain;
- no slot overlaps that route;
- each spawn exit opens into a hardened vestibule that blocks fire from outside the base.

Cost: more hardened geometry, and some permanent, predictable lanes. Letting attackers repair in a base where they hold a point would also work, but has wider effects and is not recommended.

**Settled by.** The offline check over the seven blueprints. Soak: time any point spends unreachable from its base boundary in navigation (target zero); stuck time near points.

**Verifiers.** Both confirmed major. The Stage 1 case is weaker than written: defender engineer bots order repairs by distance from points and spawn exits (§6.11), and bots will not isolate a point on purpose. The major rating rests on Stage 2, where the owner gains by not repairing and D-23's Consequence claims the opposite.

#### L5-02 (+L1-V1) — D-28 does not say what happens when two attackers split the points, or whether a lost spawn comes back

*Both · major · structural · decide before M6 · feeds O-20*

**Claim.** D-17, D-28 and §6.3 all say "the attackers", and nothing defines that for two attacking factions. §6.4's three-way rule covers only the timer. "Until" can mean a one-time loss or a condition re-checked every tick.

**Scenario.** F1 owns C. F2 holds A and B, and F3 flips the third point.

- Reading 1, attackers count together: F1's spawn closes.
- Reading 2, one faction must hold every point: F1 keeps spawning into a base where it holds nothing, for as long as F2 and F3 split it. That inverts §4's claim that whoever looks strongest suffers from being attacked from two directions.

Separately, F3 sweeps 1·3, and a faction-1 medic bot that survived upstairs revives two defenders and re-flips the point at 20:25, inside the 45 s hold.

- If the spawn returns at once, reinforcement arrives in 16.5 s, and one survivor can undo any sweep.
- If it does not, the deploy screen shows a base that F1 owns and cannot spawn into, with no rule for when it reopens.

**Evidence.** All three factions can attack C from the first tick (§5.1), so a split is routine. Reinforcement takes 16.5 s, against holds of 45 s and 75 s.

**Recommendation.** Write the rule from the owner's side, so that it never has to count attackers:

- the owner can spawn at a base while it holds at least one of its points;
- the spawn closes when it holds none, and reopens only when the capture timer has drained to zero (L1-V1's wording, which keeps D-28's priced intent that the sweep is decisive);
- a soldier counting down to a spawn that closes or changes owner returns to the deploy screen with the countdown already served.

L5's alternative, reopening on a re-flip, lengthens attacks and adds to L1-01's handicap, so it is not recommended while L1-01 stands. Cost: a third faction can close an owner's spawn without making any progress of its own, which is the pressure from two directions that §4 asks for.

**Settled by.** The owner's ruling. Then, in soak: spawn-close events in which more than one non-owner held the points; share of sweeps reversed before the timer completes, by base size; time from a point being retaken to the first defender respawn there.

**Verifiers.** L5's verifier confirmed major. L1-V1 was raised by L1's verifier, is confirmed here against D-17, D-28, §6.3 and §6.4, and is merged in; its 1–1 split half is in L1-07.

#### L3-05 — Nobody owns the faction pool

*Both · major · structural · decide before M7 · feeds O-23*

**Claim.** Any placement spends from the faction pool (§6.7), repair is paid per voxel, the commander buys modules from the same pool (§6.11), and D-15 gives the human no orders. Nothing says who may spend, whether the commander can reserve funds, or whether a human can be refused. Tuning cannot remove the contest: a pool slack enough never to be contended leaves D-25's income lever and D-32's bet with nothing to act on.

**Scenario.** Stage 1, with illustrative numbers: the human engineer deploys to 1·2 ahead of F2's attack and finds the pool at 40 with the cheapest module at 100, because a second earlier the commander started a watchtower at 1·3. Or the reverse: they spend the pool on bunkers at quiet 1·3, and the wall the commander planned for 1·2 cannot be bought. Stage 2: one player places every module they can afford in a base with ten seconds left on its timer, and D-32 crumbles the lot.

**Evidence.** If classes split evenly there are about 11 bot engineers per faction (33 / 3), and a commander that replans once a second. Rainbow Six Siege moved to a shared reinforcement pool of 10 in September 2020, shared by five teammates. PlanetSide 2's Cortium is harvested and spent by one player or squad.

**Recommendation.** Spend through allowances:

- each engineer, bot or human, draws a build allowance from the pool when deploying or at a terminal, up to a personal cap, and places and repairs from it;
- the commander directs spending through where it sends bot engineers, and may earmark part of the income for a base;
- the human draws on the same terms as a bot, and the commander cannot pre-empt their allowance.

Income and the director's lever are unchanged. Cost: the commander can no longer pour the whole pool into one base in a second, and the HUD shows an allowance beside the pool. A griefer can waste only their own allowance.

**Settled by.** Playtest: share of the human's build attempts refused for lack of funds, and how often a module the commander planned goes unbought because the human spent first. In soak, a scripted engineer that spends greedily stands in for the human.

**Verifier.** Confirmed major for Stage 1. A human who spends first delays the commander's next purchase rather than wrecking its plan, and the Stage 2 griefing half is not a one-way door. Siege's comparable cuts both ways: Ubisoft shared the pool to spread effort, and PC Gamer judged trolling it "high-effort/low payoff", but with five sharers, not 100–200.

### Both stages — minor

#### L1-05 (+L4-03) — No engagement envelope, and perception has no range or visibility term

*Both · minor · tuning · decide before M3*

The ranges the fight depends on are never stated:

- §6.2's "medium range" is undefined.
- §6.9 gives no damage falloff, maximum range or projectile lifetime. Uncapped reach at 600 m/s is about 37 km, and the drop over 450 m is only 2.8 m.
- §6.11's perception is a binary view cone with no distance or visibility term, and the distance fog (§7.6) does not enter line of sight.
- R26's chain names a longest engagement range that nobody gives.

The human crossing 450 m of grass towards 3·1 can therefore be engaged from a watchtower 400 m away, where they are about 5 px tall, and arrive without shields, never having seen the shooter.

Add a line to §6.9 stating the infantry envelope as data: full damage to X m, minimum damage at Y m, a projectile lifetime, a longest engagement range (proposed 200–300 m), and whether the ground between bases is meant to be a firing range. Cap perception at that range, and make noticing build up over time, scaled by angular size, motion, muzzle flash, fog and angle from the view centre. Mark the killer's position in the HUD on death. Give every lattice link a covered approach. For Stage 2, propose R26 per class of entity as a C-nn.

Settled by: the distribution of shooter-to-target distance for hits and kills; the share of attacker deaths outside base bounds; the share of the human's deaths to unseen killers.

Both verifiers downgraded to minor tuning:

- Spread, bloom, aiming down sights and aim error are already data. §6.11's aim error must close about 12 times tighter at 300 m than at 25 m, so time to first hit already grows with range, and a perception range is one more value.
- R26 holds trivially in Stage 1, since every entity is sent (§7.4) and terrain renders to 2 km (§7.6).
- The extra one-way delay applies only under the conditioner; Stage 1 runs over loopback.
- A soldier at 300 m is 7.7 px tall at 1440p, not 8.4.

#### L2-04 — Nothing in the design defines C's role

*Both · minor · tuning · decide before M6*

When two attacking factions stand on one point they freeze each other (§6.4). Who holds C is set by how commanders value it, which §6.11 does not say. §5.1 calls C "a contested middle", but nothing makes anyone contest it.

In L2's model, leader-biased commanders held C for 83–95% of two hours, and C changed hands only once.

The owner decides C's role in §5.1: a permanent three-way grinder or a rotating prize. Give the commander a target term for C (L2-02), and add the §9 line that no base is held continuously for more than 60 minutes.

Downgraded to minor tuning. The verifier's re-run shows the long holds come from commanders never attacking C, not from the freeze: an owner-only freeze left them unchanged (85–97%). The freeze matters only when both enemies commit to C, where it halves the holder's share (80% to 40%). The opening race's winner also differed from seed to seed.

#### L2-08 (+L4-04, L6-02) — §4 promises a war "already running" that "will keep running when they quit"; every session opens on the same board

*Both · minor · structural · decide before M6*

The client launches the server at Play (§4, §7.1), the job object kills it on exit, D-11 keeps nothing, §5.1 fixes every base's starting owner, and the human is always faction 1. Every session therefore opens with the same race for C; the first capture lands at about 125–136 s in every seed. Nothing says what the seed varies (§7.2).

Correct §4 now. Then add variety cheaply:

1. Seeded doctrine per faction per session, within bounds: aggression, fortifying against attacking, C against the flanks, garrison size.
2. Squads choose among the k cheapest routes within X% of the best, using the seeded PRNG.
3. A varied start: either a seeded warm-up of a few minutes before the deploy screen opens (at a mean tick of about 4 ms against 33 ms, 5 minutes takes about 40 s; an estimate), or several authored start boards in the map file.

Optionally supersede D-11 narrowly with "continue the war or start a new one". The late-join chunk diff (§7.4), plus ownership, timers, pools and director state, is a complete save.

Settled by: across sessions, whether by session 3 the owner can predict each enemy's first objective and entry route; across seeds, the entropy of route choice and the variance of territory share at 30 minutes.

L2-08 was confirmed minor. L4-04 was downgraded, because a skill distribution (§6.11), a seeded PRNG (§7.2) and a seed per session (§4, §7.1) already exist and a 99-bot war diverges within minutes; only the openings repeat. L6-02 was downgraded, because the director, two-front pressure, D-32 and restoration pull a restored war back towards the start, and a save can be added at any milestone.

#### L5-03 — The lattice rule does not define "attacked"

*Both · minor · structural · decide before M6*

"A base can be attacked only by a faction that owns a base linked to it." Flipping, freezing, counting towards a majority and running a timer are separate acts, and only the last is clearly covered. "Any enemy presence" freezes a point with no link condition, and nothing says whether a link is checked during a capture or only when it starts.

Add to §6.4: only a faction owning a node linked to the base can flip, freeze or hold its points. Other factions may fight and destroy voxels there but are invisible to the capture rules. A warpgate is a node its faction always owns. Links are checked every tick, and an attacker that loses its last link loses its majority, so its progress drains.

Downgraded to minor; the claim that a faction pushed back to its warpgate can never attack again is refuted (see §9 of this report). In Stage 1, commanders plan along links, so presence by a faction with no link happens only by accident. PlanetSide 2 also requires a lattice link to capture.

#### L5-11 — The rules for a single control point are incomplete

*Both · minor · structural · decide before M6*

Nothing says:

- whether partial flip progress persists, decays or resets when the flipper leaves or the point freezes;
- whether the owner standing on a partly flipped point reverses it;
- how three factions share one point;
- what the rate is for two or three soldiers.

§15's "holds by standing on it" is looser than §6.4's flip, which persists; §6.4 governs. A lone human can pre-cap C's three points to 11 of 12 s each and let their squad sweep them in seconds.

Add to §6.4: a flipped point stays held until it is flipped. Flip progress belongs to one faction at a time; it decays at the flip rate when none of that faction is on the point; a different faction drains it before building its own; and the owner standing uncontested drains it at its own rate. Change §15 to "takes by standing on it". Confirmed minor.

#### L3-08 (+L5-08) — Repair and restoration can seal a soldier inside a thick wall

*Both · minor · structural · decide before M7*

§6.7 spares only the voxel a soldier "is standing in", and frame building has no such clause at all. A soldier left in a cavity the shape of their own box cannot move, and only the downed may respawn (§6.2). In a two-hour soak, 1% of bot-time is 118.8 bot-minutes (99 bots × 120 minutes), so one bot sealed in during the first minute fails §9 on its own.

Repair, restoration and frame building should leave unfilled any gap of missing blueprint voxels that a soldier's box overlaps, living or downed; at minimum, any voxel within one voxel of a box. Redeploy (L1-06) is the general escape from any stuck state.

Settled by: the count of soldiers fully enclosed by solid voxels.

Both minor. L5's verifier swept the box through walls 2, 3 and 4 voxels thick: sealing is possible only in a 2 m wall breached clean through. Restoration needs no enemy presence, so it can entomb a soldier only in their own faction's base; the attacker case needs an enemy engineer who repairs around them rather than shooting them.

#### L5-09 — How grenades and breach charges are replenished is unruled

*Both · minor · tuning · decide before M7*

§6.5 does not say whether the engineer's ammo pack, a terminal, a respawn or a cooldown refills explosives. Repair costs pool per voxel, while breaching costs nothing.

Add to §6.5: counts held in data; a terminal and a respawn restore them; the ammo pack restores bullets and grenades but not breach charges. Alternatively, give charges a pool cost. Breach-edge costs then read the squad's remaining charges.

Downgraded to minor tuning. Refill amounts per source are values in data, the cost asymmetry holds under every reading, and free restoration means a defender is never forced to pay for a breach.

#### L5-10 — Crumble damage has no instigator, and the bots' blast check ignores their own modules

*Both · minor · structural · decide before M7*

Two things remain open:

- whose instigator crumble damage carries (R27), and whether it follows the friendly-fire flag;
- whether D-31's bot check covers the voxels an edit would crumble, and its own faction's module voxels. Bot frags otherwise clear their own cover (L3-04).

Add to §6.8: crumbling is part of the edit that caused it, carries that edit's instigator, and follows the same friendly-fire flag. A bot's ally check covers the blast and the crumble, and refuses an explosion that would remove its own faction's module voxels unless its squad chose that breach.

Downgraded to minor and moved from M4 to M7. Whether explosions spare their own faction's walls is already answered: §6.8 has no faction condition, §7.4's operation carries no faction, and §6.7's "enemy damage" presupposes that a faction can damage its own base.

### Stage 2 — minor

#### L6-07 — A Windows-only dedicated server raises a solo developer's hosting bill

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before M1*

D-01, D-12 and §7.4 make the server a Windows binary. On AWS the Windows licence is $0.046 per vCPU-hour, about $537 a month for a 16-vCPU server. Cloud egress is larger again, all estimates at 150 kbit/s average and 40% utilisation: about 5.8 TB and $525 a month at 300 clients, and 11.7 TB and $1,040 at 600. Bare metal with unmetered traffic removes the egress bill.

Do not build a Linux server now. Record in ADR-001 that the server, and every library it links, reaches the operating system only through the platform and transport layer; AGENTS.md §4 already routes `<windows.h>` through one header.

Confirmed minor, with corrected numbers. Hetzner's own price list puts Windows Server Standard at €27.90 a month for 8 cores, and the 8-core AX42 at about €97.30 after the June 2026 increase. Windows adds about 29% on bare metal, not 50–120%.

#### L6-05 — Humans take away the director's strongest lever

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before M6*

The director's soldier bounds allow at most a 39/27 = 1.44 times gap. A 600-player continent split 40/33/27 is already 1.48 times, and 45/30/25 is 1.80 times. Topping up the smallest faction with bots costs, by estimate, up to 13 ms of tick time for 78 bots.

Run two configuration-only soaks at M6:

- the soldier lever frozen, at 39/33/27;
- one faction's bot skill distribution shifted up by one standard deviation.

If either fails §9, flow comes from the director's headcount rather than from the lattice, and Stage 2 needs a population mechanism. Write the joining rule now (L5-12).

Downgraded to minor: caps, assigning joiners to the smallest faction and backfill are all additive. PlanetSide 2 lowered its per-faction continent cap from 300 to 250 in November 2022, and assigns its Freelancers (NSO) to the faction with the lowest population. The first soak variant mostly answers a Stage 1 question, which makes it worth running anyway.

#### L4-08 (+L7-01) — Whether bots are the live game's population is unstated, and the population arithmetic says they will be

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before Stage 2*

§2 maps "1 human + 99 bots" to "Real multiplayer", and nothing says whether bots stay. The arithmetic says they must:

- With an estimated peak-to-trough ratio of 2.5–4, holding a 300-human floor through the daily trough in two regions needs 1,050–1,500 average concurrent players. That is 2.6–3.8 times PlanetSide 2's current Steam average.
- That average fell from about 700 (January 2025) to about 424 (June 2026) and about 372 (August 2026). These are Steam-only figures.
- PlanetSide Arena peaked at 1,481 and closed in January 2020.
- BattleBit's recent daily peaks are 0.53–1.2% of its 87,323 launch peak.

State in §1 or §6.11 whether bots are intended as Stage 2's permanent backfill. If they are:

- commanders and squads model soldiers they do not control (L4-01);
- the soldier lever adds and removes bots only at death or respawn (L5-12);
- the net-ready test has the second client take a bot's seat and then leave, with flow unchanged;
- the flow soak also runs at 45 and 150 soldiers; §7.1 already calls a design that works only at 100 a defect.

Be open about the human count.

Both were downgraded to minor. §2's "Later" column lists additions and removes nothing. Most of the hand-over already exists: the soldier lever adds and removes bots, the human already fills one of faction 1's 33, and R19 drives humans and bots through one command stream. Battlefield 6 caps bot backfill in Verified Portal servers at 12 per match, which suggests players accept bots as a minority that gives up seats. That is a positioning risk for declaring a permanent bot majority.

#### L6-01 — No seam for human leadership beside the AI commander

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before Stage 2*

The commander owns objectives, squad assignment and module purchasing (§6.11). §6.13 reserves seams only for possession and more humans. When human leaders arrive, nothing says who wins when one disagrees with the commander.

Make the commander's output an explicit, replicated record of faction orders, each carrying an author id: objective, intent, priority, squads assigned and build budget. In the MVP it also shows the human their faction's plan (L4-01).

Downgraded to minor. The commander is server-only AI with no protocol or data commitment (§7.1), so precedence is a Stage 2 rule. The pool contest in Stage 1 is L3-05.

#### L6-04 — Relevance without a line-of-sight filter hands a live game's wallhack the whole build-and-breach picture

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before Stage 2*

§7.4 sends every entity within the relevance radius, and §8 has no encryption or anti-cheat. A passive packet reader therefore sees the squad stacked behind a wall for a breach. A precompiled visibility set cannot work in a destructible world. Bandwidth is also at stake: by estimate, 200 relevant entities at 15–30 updates a second is 240–480 kbit/s against the 256 kbit/s target.

Build perception as a visibility service per observer, and in Stage 2 switch relevance by policy to "visible, audible or soon visible".

Downgraded to minor. The primitives are already required (the §7.1 partition and §7.3 grid rays). Perception (a view cone with a reaction time) is a different query from a conservative, all-round relevance filter. The Stage 2 cost, about 1.2 million rays a second at 600 × 200 × 10 Hz (an estimate), belongs to the concept, not to any MVP decision. The claim that R26 survives such a filter is false for grenades, which bounce (§7.3), and for projectiles with drop (§6.9).

#### L6-06 — §1's scale proof covers one client: bots have no sockets

*Stage 2 · minor · structural · decide before Stage 2*

§1 says a hundred bots exercise interest management and edit replication "exactly as a hundred humans would". But a bot has no socket (§6.11). Per-client relevance, delta snapshots and reliable operation queues (§7.4) therefore run for one human and at most one verification client (§7.10).

Correct §1's sentence. Add a virtual-client soak mode, in which bots get server-side client records whose packets go to a null sink, and add to §9 a p99 tick target with 100 virtual clients.

Downgraded to minor. Work for each client is independent and parallelises: 600 clients × 30 snapshots a second × about 20 µs is about 0.36 core-seconds per second (an estimate). The mode can be added at any milestone.

#### L7-05 — The working title is the name of a failed 2011 MMO

*Stage 2 · minor · tuning · decide before Stage 2*

Masthead Studios released Earthrise in February 2011 and shut its servers on 9 February 2012. SilentFuture's revival reached open alpha in May 2014 and was last seen in 2017. D-35 already makes a rename free in code. Settle the public name and run a trademark search before any public page; O-14 covers faction names, not the title.

Confirmed minor. The verifier moved the deadline from M6 to Stage 2, since no milestone involves a public page.

## 5. Decision challenges

**D-17 — spawn at the warpgate or an owned base (L1-01).** Failure: an attacker's death costs about 80 s and a defender's about 16 s, so contested attacks need 1.65–2.2 times the garrison and a human attacker spends half to two-thirds of their time out of contact. Why it is unpriced: D-17's Consequence names only the defenders' rule, and §5.1 treats the walk as a symmetric cost. Cheapest alternative: a staging spawn at each link end, 125–150 m from the opposite base, with D-28 kept. Verdict: major, put to the owner as O-16. The verifier removed the blocker rating, because concentrated attacks still finish, but kept the structural core.

**D-09 — endless sandbox, no win state (L2-06, L6-03).** Failure: no session reaches an acknowledged ending; §9 and the director both work against any lead lasting beyond four bases; and the reference game added closure for exactly this reason. Why it is unpriced: the Consequence covers flow and acceptance, not the player's reason to stop or return. Cheapest alternative: a session summary at quit; then, if playtests ask for it, alert windows inside the endless map. Verdict: the verifiers split between major and minor. The five-session playtest settles it, and it is not raised as an open question.

**D-11 — nothing persists (L6-02, merged into L2-08).** Failure: §4 promises a war "already running" that "will keep running when they quit", and the build cannot deliver either. Why it is partly priced: D-11's Consequence prices "no save format", and the late-join chunk diff makes a save addable at any time. Cheapest alternative: correct §4 now, and consider a "continue the war" save later. Verdict: minor.

**§6.10's symmetric treatment of the human's faction (L2-05, against D-16).** Not a challenge to D-16 itself. The narrow point is that the respawn lever lands on the human's own countdown on every death while moving the war by about 2–3%. Exempting their countdown is a small owner choice. Verdict: minor.

**Decisions upheld.** Every lens that touched D-28 recommends keeping it and fixing the attacker's side instead. D-15 is not challenged: squad membership and a shared picture (L4-01, L3-10) give the human no command. D-23's Consequence, "the capture loop cannot be deleted", is not a decision to challenge, but it is true only with the access rule in L3-03.

## 6. What §9 misses

Every current §9 target can be green while attackers walk two-thirds of the time, bases change hands empty, breaches change nothing, the pool never binds and the human fights alone. The additions below make those states visible. All are computed from R27 events (§7.9), plus a periodic per-soldier contact sample and a few event fields. Targets are proposals, held as tuning data.

**Flow, over the two-hour soak:**

- Share of soldier-time in contact, split by attacker and defender. Contact means alive and within engagement range of a perceived enemy, or having dealt or taken damage in the last 5 s. Proposed: attackers at least 50%, defenders at least 60% (L1-02).
- Median time from spawn to first contact: at most 30 s (L1-02).
- Contested captures: at least 70% of captures see three or more kills inside the base while the timer runs (L2-01).
- Fight share: at least 30% of soldier-time is spent in a base where two or more factions are present (L2-01).
- Joinability: the median capture-progress window lasts at least about 165 s, or there is a ceiling of about 20 captures an hour (L2-01).
- No single base held continuously for more than 60 minutes (L2-01, L2-04).
- Breaches that mattered: in at least 25% of captures, a point is flipped by an attacker who passed through an explosion-made opening in the previous 60 s; and breaches per base attack lasting over 3 minutes, replacing breaches per hour map-wide (L3-11, L3-02).
- Modules that mattered: bases with half or more of their slots filled are captured at least 15 points less often than bare ones, and a completed module survives a median of at least 60 s under attack (L3-11).
- Pool binds: no faction's pool is at its cap, or below the cheapest module, for more than 50% of the time (L3-11, L3-06).
- The downed state: seconds a point is frozen with only downed soldiers on it (target zero, if O-17 is adopted as recommended), and mean time downed and revive rate by role (L1-04).
- Spawn safety: defender deaths within 3–5 s of spawning, per base attack (L1-03).
- Squad cohesion: attacker inter-arrival times at a point, and the share of attacking-bot deaths with fewer than two squadmates within 30 m (L4-05).
- Director: share of time active, and share of time at a bound (L2-05).
- Balance: each faction's mean territory share within ±5 points across seeds run without a human (L2-07).
- Ability use per class per hour, with a real value for the engineer (L5-14).
- The same flow targets at 45 and 150 soldiers, and with the soldier lever frozen at 39/33/27 (L4-08, L6-05).
- Server tick p99 with 100 virtual clients (L6-06).

**Play, from logged sessions of the owner and from a soak player-proxy:**

- The human's time alive in contact: at least 50% over an hour (L1-02).
- Time from deploy to first engagement, and the distribution of life lengths (L4-07).
- Share of deaths to a killer never in their view with line of sight for at least 0.5 s before the first hit (L1-05).
- Time within 50 m of at least three allies sharing their objective (L4-01).
- Bot breaches and module completions witnessed within about 100 m, per hour (L3-01, L3-02).
- Where and why they stop, across five sessions (L2-06).

## 7. Unresolved

**One contradiction was settled by arithmetic.** L1 argues that defence is too strong; L2-01 and L4-09 argue that captures come too easily, by ghost-capping. They are one mechanism. Defence wins contested fights (parity 1.65–2.2 times by L1's model, a capture cliff at about twice the garrison in L2's), so captures come from garrisons left under-committed. Both lenses are right, and the fix belongs on the attacker's side (L1-01) and in commander policy (L2-02).

**A second contradiction was settled by D-28's priced intent.** L5-02 proposed reopening a lost spawn on a re-flip; L1-V1 proposed reopening it only when the timer drains. A re-flip reopening lets one survivor undo any sweep, since reinforcement arrives in 16.5 s against holds of 45 s and 75 s. That widens L1-01 and contradicts D-28's "decisive moment". The drain wording is recommended (O-20).

The rest paper could not settle:

**The war's regime.** Whether a band of commander garrison values exists where captures are both fought and above 6 an hour, and whether the director keeps 5-of-7 holds under 15 minutes there. L2's model says such a band exists at garrisons of about 2–8 of 33. Its verifier re-ran the model and found holds of 72–85 minutes with no director at garrisons of 8–9, and 45–49 minutes with the director at its bounds, which contradicts L2's strength "no lasting steamroll". The verifier also found that the model freezes a whole base whenever any second faction is anywhere inside it, which overstates defence. Settled by an M6 soak sweeping the garrison fraction: captures per hour, contested share, fight share, the longest 5-of-7 hold, and the director's share of time at a bound.

**Whether the sweep should be final.** Once a lost spawn reopens only on drain, the owner still has to choose whether the hold after a sweep is long enough for a counter-push from outside: about 95–105 s effective, against 45 s and 75 s now. L2's model puts the cost at roughly 30 to 12 captures an hour at a 6-soldier garrison when nominal small and medium timers rise to 230 s. Settled by soak (timers reversed by base size, captures per hour, under both settings) and by playtest (whether the human feels they can save a base).

**Spawn-room camping.** The L1 verifier rates it minor, the L5 and L4 verifiers major (L1-03). Settled by soak (defender deaths within 3–5 s of spawning, per base attack) and by playtest (the share of the human's deploys at an attacked base that end within 5 s).

**Closure.** The L2 verifier rates it minor, the L6 verifier major (L2-06). Settled by a playtest over five or more sessions: where the owner stops, why, and whether they would play an alert window through.

**Redeploy.** Free redeploy brings defenders to an attacked base in 12–17 s and widens L1-01; no redeploy leaves walking, or a self-frag if D-31 covers the thrower. The recommendation pairs a penalised redeploy with staging spawns (L1-06). Settled by soak: defender arrivals per minute at an attacked base, and contact share by side, with and without redeploy.

**Whether bots out-see the human at range.** The lens said no single reaction value works. The verifier showed the stated aim model already scales time to hit with the target's angular size (L1-05). Settled by playtest: the share of unseen-killer deaths, and median first-detection distance, bot on human against human on bot.

**Whether regrouping raises or lowers captures per hour.** Waves concentrate pressure, but waiting lengthens the cycle (L4-05). Settled by soak: captures per hour and lone deaths, with and without the regroup rule.

## 8. Strengths to protect

**D-28 makes defending a high-contact experience,** about 70–80% of a defender's time at lives of 40–60 s, and makes the sweep a single, legible climax. A plausible fix for L1-01 from the defenders' side, such as losing the spawn at the first point or adding defender respawn penalties, would cut defender contact to the attacker's level. The fix belongs on the attacker's side.

**Friendly fire from explosives only, with a bot blast check (D-31).** One human among 32 friendly bots is never killed by their aim error. Turning bullet friendly fire on through the data flag "for realism", or dropping the bots' check to save server time in heavy fights, would break it.

**The director never touches damage, health or accuracy (§6.10).** Fights stay honest while the war is steered. That line needs defending exactly when the flow metrics are red and a hidden bonus for the underdog, or a bot accuracy lever, is the quickest knob.

**A revive is worth about 80 s to an attacker and about 16 s to a defender.** That makes the medic the attacker's key class, and gives the attack a tool that scales with its handicap. Cheap revive denial, or a shorter window, would remove the attacker's main answer to the walk.

**Capture progress belongs to one attacker at a time (§6.4).** Two rivals cannot pool a timer against a holder, and the rule is simple enough to read off the HUD. Fixing C's role (L2-04) by moving to parallel progress per faction would break it.

**Territory buys little.** A capture keeps the damage and resets the modules (D-32 with §6.7), so the new owner inherits its own breaches and starts from a bare shell. On this lattice nearly every owned base is a front, so income per attackable base is 1.0–1.5 times within §9's limits, and every base gained is one more to hold with the same 33 soldiers. Ties between soldier count or spawn distance and territory, warpgate income, modules surviving a capture, or a larger Stage 2 lattice with many rear bases would weaken it. It is not sufficient on its own: the model still produced long 5-of-7 holds near the garrison knee.

**Binary, stateless voxel removal (§5.4).** A wall is either there or holed, with no hidden health, and every heavy wall is a plain choice between one assault's charge and going round. Per-voxel damage for Stage 2 vehicles, or several explosive types able to remove heavy material, would blur it.

**Breaching is a costed navigation edge that repair closes (§6.11, §7.5).** The build–breach duel falls out of pathfinding, and bots and the human use one mechanic. Replacing it with scripted breach events under time pressure would break it; authored breach spots (L3-01) must stay edges.

**A bot is a client without a socket (R19, R20).** Every bot exercises the human's code paths and is an honest client that can backfill a live server. An unstick teleport, bots spawned straight onto objectives, engineers writing voxels directly, or AI reading global truth to save CPU when the 8.3 ms budget tightens would break it.

**Voxel edits replicate as operations, and a late joiner receives the chunks that differ from blueprint (§7.4).** That makes save, restore and restart recovery nearly free. Replicating results instead of operations, replaying the whole operation log to late joiners, or changing blueprints without versioning would break it.

**One server process per continent, from the same binary as the client-hosted server (D-01, D-36).** This is the simplest operating model a solo developer could have. Splitting the simulation across processes, or adding a backend service that the game loop depends on, would end it.

**Server-authoritative hits (D-10, R18), and §6.9's written-down choice not to rewind the world.** They remove the hit-claim cheats that client-side detection invites, and close a hit-against-voxel timing case on paper. A client-side shortcut "for feel", a longer rewind cap for high-latency players, or rewinding voxels for hit tests would reopen them.

**N-faction data and a rotationally symmetric lattice (D-05, R23, §5.1).** Every node and link maps onto another under the rotation, so no faction's rules differ by position. An `isEnemy` shortcut, a link added for flow without its two rotations, or a §10 cut applied to one faction's bases would break it.

**Construction only on slots inside the lattice bases being fought over, funded by territory, with no automated defences (§6.7, D-25, O-15).** This is what PlanetSide 2's 2023 construction overhaul aimed for: bases that non-builders enjoy fighting at. §2's "free-form building, harvesting", if it moves building away from the fight, or turret modules added to the library would undo it.

**The narrow destruction model is affordable at scale.** Explosives only, an operation of about 16 bytes, no rigid bodies and cosmetic debris (§6.8, §7.3, §7.4) cost little at 100 soldiers, and will at 300–600. Debris that affects gameplay, per-voxel damage, or physics collapse added for spectacle would turn operations into replicated results.

**The support check is bounded by a voxel count, not a time slice (§6.8).** The overhang it accepts is deterministic and needs no replication. Turning the bound into a per-tick time budget, or spreading one check across ticks differently on client and server, would break determinism.

## 9. Refuted

**Findings.**

- **L6-08** — 0.5 m stair-step terrain is hostile to the long-term game's ground vehicles. Refuted: vehicles are excluded (§8) and listed under Later (§2), and the lens's own fix (hover vehicles or a smoothed collision proxy) shows D-20 is not a one-way door.
- **L7-03** — one map, and no way for players to make another. Refuted: it is not a one-way door, as the lens itself said. The game reads only formats it defines and ADR-012 has not chosen them; R14 binds what ships, not a later R14-clean mod tool. The concern that one map is soon learned belongs to L2-08.
- **L7-04** — §2's "Later" construction leads back to PlanetSide 2's split between builders and fighters. Refuted: the scenario needs building away from the lattice bases, which §6.7, D-25 and §8 rule out. The caution survives as a strength.

**Claims within surviving findings, so they are not raised again.**

- "Contested attacks do not finish" (L1-01, as a blocker): concentrated attacks at 2.5–3 times the garrison finish.
- A faction pushed back to its warpgate can never attack again, and earns nothing (L5-03): §15 calls a warpgate a home base, and §5.1 gives it two links. A warpgate has no build slots, so zero income is moot.
- A frag at one support drops a watchtower (L3-04): the support check is connectivity to an anchor (§6.8), and a tower on several legs survives one cut leg.
- Explosions might spare their own faction's voxels, so the operation format is open before M4 (L5-10): §6.8 and §7.4 are already faction-blind.
- A support check that gives up leaves an inconsistency for a later edit (an L5 hypothesis): each edit has one deterministic answer.
- Repair can seal a soldier in a 1 m wall (L5-08): only a 2 m wall breached clean through can do it.
- The freeze rule makes C a session-long lock (L2-04): the long holds come from commanders never attacking C.
- Engagement density "halves" at 300 players on an 8 × 8 km continent (L6): density per front link falls by a quarter, from 16.7 to 12.5 soldiers.
- Windows adds 50–120% to a bare-metal server (L6-07): about 29%.
- PlanetSide 2's Steam average was "~346 in October 2026" (L7-01): no such figure was found; the latest is about 372, for August 2026.
- Battlefield 6 froze player stats to stop farming on bot servers (L7-01): the freeze was reported as unintended. The 10% XP for bot kills holds.
- Ravenfield is "~93% positive" (L7-02): Steam rates it Overwhelmingly Positive, which means at least 95%.
